A [[stomping|stomp]] takes hit points by how long the player has been falling. Most monsters' stomps take 1, and
1 more for every 16 steps of falling. A [[yeti|Yeti]]'s, the [[yeti-king|Yeti King]]'s and a
[[shopkeeper|Shopkeeper]]'s take 1 for every 16 steps, rounded up, which is 0 when he hasn't been falling at all:
when he stomps one while still rising.

## What happens

The player jumps at a Yeti, and his feet catch its head while he is still rising: the game counts a stomp, which
stuns the Yeti, but it keeps all five of its hit points <Cite clip="quirks/yeti-stomp-rising" />.

<Clip id="quirks/yeti-stomp-rising" />

The same rounding shows at exactly 16 steps of falling. The player hops off a ledge and lands on a Yeti on his
16th step: it loses one of its five hit points, where the same drop onto a [[caveman|Caveman]] takes two of its
three <Cite clip="quirks/yeti-stomp-sixteen-steps" />.

<Clip id="quirks/yeti-stomp-sixteen-steps" />

## Why

The Yeti's stomp, which the Yeti King's and the Shopkeeper's copy, takes `ceil(fallTimer/16)` hit points, three
times that in Spike Shoes:

```gml objects/oYeti/collision_oCharacter.gml:6-13
else if (not other.dead and not other.stunned and (other.state == 15 or other.state == 16) and other.y < y+5 and not other.swimming)
{
    if (status < STUNNED)
    {
        other.yVel=-6-0.2*other.yVel
        if (global.hasSpikeShoes) { hp -= (3 * ceil(other.fallTimer/16)); instance_create(other.x, other.y+8, oBlood); }
        else hp -= (1 * ceil(other.fallTimer/16));
        other.fallTimer = 0;
```

The Yeti King's and the Shopkeeper's are the same <Cite gml="objects/oYetiKing/collision_oCharacter.gml:6-11" />
<Cite gml="objects/oShopkeeper/collision_oCharacter.gml:5-12" />. Most other monsters' stomp takes
`floor(fallTimer/16)+1` <Cite gml="objects/oEnemy/collision_oCharacter.gml:9-10" />. `fallTimer` counts the
player's steps of falling, and his step sets it to 0 on every step he isn't falling
<Cite gml="objects/oPlayer1/step.gml:254-256" /> <Cite gml="objects/oPlayer1/step.gml:290-292" />, but a stomp
needs only the player jumping (`state` 15) or falling (16). So a stomp while he's still rising, his middle less than 5
pixels below the Yeti's top (the Yeti King's 8), takes 0. At an exact multiple of 16 steps it takes a step's worth less
than other monsters' stomps: 1, not 2, after 16 steps; at any other length the two agree <Cite quirk="1" />.

## Reproducing it

Jump into a Yeti so that the player's feet reach its head on the way up. The replay of the first clip's staged
scene, on a custom level, has the player jump as a Yeti walks up to him: he stomps it on the way up, and it's
stunned with all 5 hit points <Cite replay="fidelity/replays/enemy-yeti-stomp-rising.txt" />. The second's has
him land on a Yeti on his 16th step of falling, which takes it from 5 hit points to 4, and the same drop onto a
Caveman takes it from 3 to 1 <Cite replay="fidelity/replays/enemy-yeti-stomp-sixteen-steps.txt" />.

## In the port

The spelunky.ai port reproduces it exactly: it's in docs/quirks.md's list of the 1.1 source's bugs
<Cite quirk="1" />, and the oracle's fingerprints of both replays pin the hit points. No option turns it off.
