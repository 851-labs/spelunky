A [[stomping|stomp]] is meant to be a landing on a monster's head. But the touch code most monsters share
doesn't ask whether the player is coming down: jumping counts as well as falling, as long as he's higher than
the monster's middle. So jumping up into a monster from just below kills it as a stomp, and he bounces off it.

## What happens

The player jumps up into a flying [[bat|Bat]] from just below it and kills it, and bounces higher
<Cite clip="bat/jump-kill" />.

<Clip id="bat/jump-kill" />

## Why

When a monster that inherits `oEnemy`'s touch code touches the player within 12 pixels of its middle across,
the first test is the stomp's. It accepts the player's `JUMPING` state (15) as well as `FALLING` (16), and only asks
that his `y` be above the monster's `y+8`:

```gml objects/oEnemy/collision_oCharacter.gml:6-12
else if (not other.dead and (other.state == 15 or other.state == 16) and other.y < y+8 and not other.swimming)
{
    other.yVel = -6 - 0.2 * other.yVel;
    if (global.hasSpikeShoes) { hp -= (3 * (floor(other.fallTimer/16)+1)); scrCreateBlood(other.x, other.y+8, 1); }
    else hp -= (1 * (floor(other.fallTimer/16)+1));
    other.fallTimer = 0;
    playSound(global.sndHit);
```

A player rising into a monster from just below is jumping, so it's a stomp: the monster loses a hit point (three
with [[spike-shoes|Spike Shoes]]), and the player is bounced up, `yVel` -6 less a fifth of what it was (after his
own step's gravity, which comes first). A Bat has
1 hit point, and its step kills it once they're gone <Cite gml="objects/oBat/step.gml:8" /> <Cite quirk="1" />.

## Reproducing it

Jump up into a Bat, or a [[spider|Spider]] dropping from the ceiling, from just below, so that the player's middle
is above the monster's when they touch. It happens in normal play. The replay of the clip jumps on frame 1073
and, still rising, hits a Bat: on 1076 the stomp's bounce, after his step's gravity, takes the player's `yVel`
from -3.4 to -5.4, and the Bat dies on 1077 <Cite replay="fidelity/replays/enemy-mines-seed-1-kills.txt" />.

## In the port

The spelunky.ai port reproduces it exactly: it's in docs/quirks.md's list of the 1.1 source's bugs
<Cite quirk="1" />, and the oracle's fingerprint of the replay pins the jump, the bounce and the kill. No option
turns it off.
