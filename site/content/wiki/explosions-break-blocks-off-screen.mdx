A [[bomb|Bomb]] that goes off out of sight breaks the [[block|blocks]] around it as one on the screen does. The code that
breaks them has a test that looks meant to spare blocks off the screen, everywhere but the
[[tutorial|Tutorial]], and the test is always passed.

## What happens

In the Temple, the player drops a bomb beside a [[damsel|Damsel]] and runs left, until the bomb is far past the
right edge of the screen. It goes off there, out of sight: the screen shakes. When he walks back, the floor where
the bomb lay is gone, and he drops into the hole <Cite clip="quirks/bomb-off-screen" />.

<Clip id="quirks/bomb-off-screen" />

## Why

An explosion breaks a block when it touches it, but only if this test passes:

```gml objects/oExplosion/collision_oSolid.gml:1-2
if (isLevel("rTutorial") or (x > view_xview[0]-16 and x < view_xview[0] + view_wview[0]+16 and
     y > view_yview[0]-16 and y < view_yview[0] + view_hview[0]+16))
```

The second half asks whether the explosion is within 16 pixels of the screen, as the [[throwing|thrown items']]
step does ([[items-freeze-off-screen]]). The first half reads as "or this is the Tutorial", but `isLevel` takes
no argument (GameMaker passes it one anyway, and nothing reads it). It asks whether the room is a level at all,
the Tutorial, any level of the game, [[olmec|Olmec]]'s lair or a [[level-editor|custom level]]
<Cite gml="scripts/isLevel.gml:7" />. So on a level the test always passes, and the explosion breaks every block
it touches but the unbreakable ones, the level's border and the blocks under its doors <Cite quirk="1" />. (The
only other room with explosions, the high scores room's [[sun-room|Sun Room]], passes the screen test anyway: its screen
doesn't move, and the room is barely taller than it <Cite gml="objects/oSunRoom/alarm_0.gml:31-36" />.) Whether `isRoom`, which does take a room's name, was meant is a guess: the screen's own
code uses `isRoom("rTutorial")` to keep the Tutorial's messages white <Cite gml="objects/oScreen/step_begin.gml:252" />.
The same mix-up is in the level's copy of that code, where it does nothing that shows: the screen's yellow
covers it.

The blast still only reaches what the game is running. Every step, the level pauses (deactivates) everything more
than 96 pixels past the screen, keeping the explosion itself going, and a paused bomb's fuse doesn't burn
<Cite gml="objects/oLevel/step.gml:64-100" />. So a bomb goes off out of sight only within that margin, or where
the level keeps more running: further to the right of and below the player, where a mistake in the same code
reaches, and around a rolling [[boulder]] or [[olmec|Olmec]]. The blocks it breaks are the ones still running.

## Reproducing it

Drop a bomb and run, so that it's out of sight but not too far when it goes off, then go back: the blocks around
it are gone. It happens on every level, with any bomb left behind, and with a dead [[fire-frog|Fire Frog]]'s.
The clip's replay drops the bomb on level 13 (frame 790) and runs left. It goes off on 909 at x 497, with the
screen's left edge at 103, 74 pixels past its right edge, and breaks six temple blocks and a stone block, the
floor it lay on among them (909-917). The player walks back and drops into the hole (1028-1037)
<Cite replay="fidelity/replays/temple-seed-2-bomb-off-screen.txt" />. In seed 9's City of Gold run, a Fire Frog
the player kills on level 7 leaves its bomb behind him, and it breaks two blocks 93 pixels past the screen's left
edge (3095-3097) <Cite replay="fidelity/replays/city-of-gold-seed-9.txt" />.

## In the port

The spelunky.ai port reproduces it exactly: it's in docs/quirks.md's list of the 1.1 source's bugs
<Cite quirk="1" />, and the oracle's fingerprint of the clip's replay pins the blocks the explosion breaks. No
option turns it off.
