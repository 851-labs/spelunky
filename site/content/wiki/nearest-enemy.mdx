The same two lines go wrong in each place: a test for a monster touching the thing, then a pick of the
monster nearest it. They usually agree, and disagree when two monsters are close together, so a
[[rock|Rock]] thrown at one can kill its neighbour, and a crouching player can lift a stunned enemy he isn't
standing over.

## What happens

The player throws a Rock up at a [[spider|Spider]] and a [[bat|Bat]] hanging side by side. It hits the
Spider, but the Bat dies <Cite clip="quirks/thrown-item-nearest-enemy" />.

<Clip id="quirks/thrown-item-nearest-enemy" />

The player stuns a [[caveman|Caveman]] and a [[yeti|Yeti]], then crouches over the Yeti to pick it up, and
lifts the Caveman beside it <Cite clip="quirks/pickup-nearest-enemy" />.

<Clip id="quirks/pickup-nearest-enemy" />

The player opens a [[chest|Chest]] beside a [[gold-bar|Gold Bar]], and its [[emerald|Emerald]] lands between him
and the bar. He steps onto the Gold Bar and collects nothing, for 5 steps, until the Emerald can be collected:
then he takes the Emerald, and the Gold Bar the step after <Cite clip="quirks/treasure-nearest-pick" />.

<Clip id="quirks/treasure-nearest-pick" />

## Why

While an item moves faster than 2 pixels a step, the code most items share looks for an enemy inside a small
box around it, then hurts the enemy nearest it, of any kind <Cite gml="objects/oItem/step.gml:256-263" />:

```gml objects/oItem/step.gml:256-263
else if (abs(xVel) > 2 or abs(yVel) > 2)
{
    if (collision_rectangle(x-2, y-2, x+2, y+2, oEnemy, 0, 0))
    {
        obj = instance_nearest(x, y, oEnemy);
        if (not obj.invincible and obj.type != "Magma Man")
        {
            obj.xVel = xVel;
```

`collision_rectangle` tests the monsters' collision boxes, but `instance_nearest` measures to their
positions, and most monsters' position is their sprite's top left corner. An item touching one monster's
right side can be nearer the next one's corner, and that one is hurt. The test for invincibility is the
nearest one's too, so an invincible monster beside the one hit spares it
<Cite quirk="1" row="A fast thrown item hurts the nearest enemy, not the one it hit." />. The
[[jar|Jar]], the [[skull|Skull]] and the dice have their own copies of the code, with the same pick
<Cite gml="objects/oJar/step.gml:106-108" /> <Cite gml="objects/oSkull/step.gml:104-106" />.

Picking an enemy up has it too: crouched with empty hands, the attack key looks for an enemy at the player's
feet, then takes the nearest one, and lifts it only if that one is stunned
<Cite gml="objects/oPlayer1/step.gml:1300-1309" />:

```gml objects/oPlayer1/step.gml:1300-1306
else if (collision_rectangle(x-8, y, x+8, y+8, oEnemy, 0, 0))
{
    obj = instance_nearest(x, y, oEnemy);
    if (obj.status >= 98 and obj.canPickUp)
    {
        holdItem = obj;
        holdItem.held = true;
```

So a live enemy beside a stunned one can keep the stunned one on the ground. Items come first, with the same
pick <Cite gml="objects/oPlayer1/step.gml:1206-1209" />
<Cite quirk="1" row="Picking up a stunned enemy takes the nearest enemy, not the one under the player." />.

Collecting treasure has it as well: the player's step looks for a treasure touching him, then takes the nearest
one, touching him or not, and collects it only if it can be collected yet, so one treasure a step at most
<Cite gml="objects/oPlayer1/step.gml:1990-1993" />. A gem fresh out of a Chest waits 20 steps before it can be
<Cite gml="objects/oEmerald/create.gml:7" />, and while it's the nearest, a treasure the player touches waits
with it <Cite quirk="1" row="Collecting treasure takes the nearest treasure, and nothing while that one can't be collected." />.

The pick works the other way too. The player's check for an [[arrow|arrow]] hitting him finds one touching
him, then tests the nearest arrow's speed, so a fast arrow does nothing while a slow one lies nearer
<Cite gml="objects/oPlayer1/step.gml:1513-1516" />, and the shared monster code's check for a
[[spear-trap|Spear Trap]]'s spears reads the nearest spears' frame <Cite gml="objects/oEnemy/step.gml:76-79" />
<Cite quirk="1" row="The arrow and spear checks take the nearest one, not the one touching." />.

## Reproducing it

Throw a Rock at one of two monsters close together, or stun two monsters side by side and crouch over the
right side of the one on the left. The replays of the clips' staged scenes pin both: the Rock touching the hanging Spider's
box and the Bat dying <Cite replay="fidelity/replays/action-throw-rock-nearest-enemy.txt" />, and the player
crouched over the stunned Yeti's right side lifting the stunned Caveman
<Cite replay="fidelity/replays/action-pickup-nearest-enemy.txt" />. In the same scenes without the Bat, the
Rock kills the Spider, and without the Caveman, the player lifts the Yeti. For treasure, open a Chest
beside a treasure that can already be collected and step onto that one while the Chest's gems wait: the third
scene's replay pins it <Cite replay="fidelity/replays/treasure-nearest-pick.txt" />.

## In the port

The spelunky.ai port reproduces it exactly: the four rows are in docs/quirks.md's list of the 1.1 source's bugs
<Cite quirk="1" />, and `instance_nearest` measures as GameMaker 8 does, to positions
<Cite quirk="5" row="instance_nearest measures to instances' positions, not their boxes" />. The arrows' and spears' version is read in the code and not yet in a replay, as it needs
two at once. No option turns it off.
