The open [[parachute|Parachute]] is torn by items that touch it, but only those moving right or down. The test
looks at the item's speed one way on each axis, so an item crossing it to the left, or straight up, passes
through, and the same item crossing it to the right tears it.

## What happens

Floating down a shaft, the player throws a [[rock|Rock]] up at the wall beside him. It bounces back left
through the canopy, which holds. In the next shaft he throws one at the other wall: it bounces back right
through the canopy, which tears away, and he drops <Cite clip="quirks/parachute-torn-moving-right" />.

<Clip id="quirks/parachute-torn-moving-right" />

## Why

The open parachute (`oParachute`) follows the player, a dome over his head
<Cite gml="objects/oParachute/step.gml:1-5" />. Its collision with an item replaces it with the spent canopy,
`oParaUsed`, which falls away:

```gml objects/oParachute/collision_oItem.gml:1-5
if (other.xVel > 0 or other.yVel > 0)
{
    instance_create(x, y, oParaUsed);
    instance_destroy();
}
```

`xVel > 0` is moving right and `yVel > 0` is moving down, so an item moving left or straight up, or not
moving, tears nothing. What reaches a dome over his head is an item falling onto it, which always tears it,
or one flying up through it, which tears it only moving right <Cite quirk="1" />.

## Reproducing it

Open the Parachute in a shaft and throw a Rock up at the wall on the player's right, so it comes back left
through the canopy: it holds. Thrown at the left wall, it comes back right and tears it. Play reaches it with
any item thrown or bounced up through an open parachute. The replay of the clip's staged
scene, on a custom level, does both: the Rock that crosses the canopy moving left and up leaves it whole until
he lands, and the one moving right and up tears it at once
<Cite replay="fidelity/replays/item-parachute-torn-moving-right.txt" />.

## In the port

The spelunky.ai port reproduces it exactly: it's in docs/quirks.md's list of the 1.1 source's bugs
<Cite quirk="1" />, and the oracle's fingerprint of the replay pins both crossings. No option turns it off.
